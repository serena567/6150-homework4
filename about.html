About Us

The origin of libraries dates back to ancient civilizations, where the earliest libraries were typically located within religious or academic institutions, used for storing and transmitting knowledge. Around 3000 BCE, the Sumerians of ancient Mesopotamia began recording information on clay tablets, creating some of the earliest libraries. The Library of Alexandria in ancient Egypt, established in the 3rd century BCE, became one of the world's foremost centers of knowledge, housing a vast collection of manuscripts and scrolls.

In the Middle Ages, libraries became an integral part of religious institutions, especially within European monasteries, where handwritten books were preserved and passed down. With the advent of the Renaissance, libraries gradually opened up to a broader public, particularly in European universities and royal courts, where libraries began to become more accessible and public-oriented. In modern times, with the rise of the printing press and the expansion of public education, libraries became even more important. In the 19th century, with the emergence of public libraries, libraries became crucial platforms for people to access knowledge and promote social development. Today, libraries are not only places for storing books but also core spaces for information exchange, cultural transmission, and educational learning.
